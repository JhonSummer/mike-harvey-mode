# Mike & Associates — Composer & Sound Brief

Oct 28, 2026 · @pipsiee

A 57-second 9:16 reel (Version C2) needs an original score, a three-note sound logo and a full sound design. The hook plays dry, music arrives on one word, and the three associates each own a note that pays off as the brand's sound logo.

## The sound logo

Each associate owns one note of a D major triad. Every time they finish a piece of work, their note chimes. At the regroup all three sound together, and on the end card they play as the logo.

| Associate | Colour | Note | Role in the film |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mike | Gold #E6AF46 | D4 (root) | Reads the new rules, checks Fiona's draft |
| Garry | Blue #6FA3E0 | F#4 (third) | Digs through court orders |
| Fiona | Coral #E88A6A | A4 (fifth) | Redlines fares, drafts and holds the note |

- **Timbre:** a soft mallet tone (celesta or felt-piano harmonic with a short bell tail) that sits clearly in the 1–3 kHz range so it survives phone speakers.
- **Logo shape:** D–F#–A as a quick rising arpeggio, then all three together as a chord, about 1.5s with a short ring-out.
- **Rule:** the notes are heard in the film before the logo appears, so the logo feels familiar the first time it is "heard".
- **Reuse:** we will use this logo in every future ad, so it should be commissioned and owned, not taken from a library.

## Music direction

One original cue, about 40 seconds of music in a 57-second film. Intimate, warm and calm, never "corporate uplifting". The silence before it is part of the score.

- **Key and tempo:** D major, 84 bpm. The sleep section moves to half-time (42 bpm feel) without changing the tempo grid.
- **Instruments:** close-miked felt piano, a warm low pad, soft sub pulse. The sleep section strips to piano and pad. Optional: a very quiet tanpura drone under the night, to place the film in India without a cliché.
- **Spirit (not to imitate):** the restraint of modern felt-piano composers such as Nils Frahm or Ólafur Arnalds.
- **Avoid:** ukulele, claps, "tech startup" builds, big drums, ticking-bomb thriller tension.

**The arc**

1. **0:00–0:18, hook: no music.** Real sounds only. The discomfort is intentional.
2. **0:18, "That doesn't have to be you": the first piano note lands exactly on "you".** This entrance is the most important moment in the score.
3. **0:20–0:33, associates:** the theme with a gentle pulse; the three notes are introduced.
4. **0:33–0:41, sleep shot:** a lullaby version of the theme, half-time, piano and pad only.
5. **0:41–0:47, morning:** music dips for the phone buzz, then returns softly.
6. **0:47–0:57, board call to end card:** the theme opens up warm, swells under "Fast. And cited." and resolves into the sound logo.

## The sleep shot (0:33–0:41)

This is the feel-good heart of the film. Rohan sleeps restlessly while the associates finish one satisfying task after another. The sound should make the viewer feel every completion as a small reward.

**The dopamine loop, repeated three times**

1. **Build:** a tiny rising swell (about 0.3s) as each card reaches its last item.
2. **Hit:** the associate's note plus one crisp, close-miked tactile sound (tag tick, overruled thunk, stamp thud).
3. **Release:** the card shrinks back with a soft air sound; the music breathes for a beat.

The notes rise across the shot (D, then F#, then A), so it feels like progress climbing. The regroup chord at 6:30 (all three notes plus a faint shimmer) is the biggest reward of the film and should feel like exhaling.

**Breathing and snoring: he snores, they chime**

Rohan's breathing is the shot's metronome, locked to the 84 bpm grid: one inhale and exhale every two beats. Each associate's note lands on his **exhale**, like a call and response.

**Snore rules**

- Exactly **one** proper snore (at 5:40). It should get a smile, not a groan: warm, a little comic, never wet or nasal.
- Interrupting it with Fiona's stamp thud is the joke: "while you were snoring, Fiona finished."
- No snore ever plays over the voiceover.
- Everything in this shot stays soft. The phone buzz at 0:41 must be the loudest sound since 0:17.

## Mix and delivery specs

The film is made for phones, mostly watched muted, so the mix must work on a phone speaker and every beat must also work with sound off.

- **Loudness:** final mix around −14 LUFS integrated, true peak no higher than −1 dBTP.
- **Voice first:** duck music 8–10 dB under voiceover and dialogue.
- **Phone speakers:** check the mix on an actual phone. Keep the associates' notes and key effects in the 1–3 kHz range; don't rely on sub-bass for anything important.
- **Mono-safe:** minimal stereo width; confirm nothing disappears when folded to mono.
- **First second:** the opening ping must be clear and immediate, for viewers who unmute.
- **Format:** 48 kHz / 24-bit WAV.
